# Resource ownership and limits

This audit began at Mu4 `afba9f0a` and includes the async-output, rate-limit cleanup, connection-admission and channel-transport changes through the 17 October 2026 transport optimizations. It traces ordinary request, response, connection and callback lifetimes. It is a code audit with bounded correctness tests, not a capacity measurement, parser review or proof that all allocation paths are bounded. See also [HTTP/2 concurrency](HTTP2-CONCURRENCY.md) and the implementation and validation record in [Nonblocking IO](NONBLOCKING-IO-INVESTIGATION.md).

## Main findings

Mu4 has useful per-request and per-connection limits, including opt-in server-wide connection admission, but **no server-wide memory budget**. The default 1,000 concurrent request slots do not cap idle sockets, sockets in setup, upgraded WebSockets, pending writes within a request, or application state retained by callbacks. Virtual threads reduce the cost of waiting; they do not put a limit on that work.

Two async-output allocation/retention problems are fixed here:

* A completed write future previously retained its payload, callback and response through the queued-write object. The public future now detaches from its response before publishing completion. Queued callbacks no longer capture the payload entry. Failures can still retain a supplied exception, and application callbacks can retain anything their own closures capture.
* Direct and read-only buffers previously caused a heap copy equal to the entire remaining payload. The socket adapter now uses at most 8 KiB of copy scratch per active write; channel response rendering uses at most 16 KiB, with one final flush. Array-backed inputs retain their direct-array path into the encoder. This bounds the copy, not the submitted payload or pending-write queue.

## Inventory

| HTTP/2 encoding/batching — `Http2FrameBuffer`, `Http2WriteBatch` | 256-byte small buffer per writer. A single logical frame can exceed the target; encodings larger than a cache lease are discarded after use. | Completion callbacks/listeners — `SerialApplicationTasks`, `ApplicationTaskContext`, `BaseResponse` | Serialized queues and application-registered listeners have no hard count cap. | Timers — `MuServerBuilder`, connection and WebSocket maintenance | Default scheduler removes cancelled tasks and disables delayed/periodic execution after shutdown. ## What this means for a 64 MiB total limit

No 64 MiB claim can be made from these defaults. Even one request may aggregate a 24 MiB input plus intermediate and decoded objects. The JVM also needs class metadata, compiled code, thread stacks, GC structures and native/TLS allocations. Heap sizing alone cannot bound total process or container memory; kernel socket accounting also depends on which limit is being measured.

Until measured on an otherwise quiet machine, use streaming, modest request/message limits, explicit connection admission in Mu and/or at the deployment boundary, and at most one outstanding async send per producer. These practices reduce avoidable growth; they are not a tested 64 MiB configuration. A future memory-envelope campaign must distinguish process RSS from container-accounted memory, include idle and active TLS/H2/WebSocket connections, and observe recovery after cancellation and disconnect.

## Follow-up priorities

1. Decide the policy for excessive live rate-limit bucket cardinality. Expired-history cleanup is implemented; it is not a cap on live history or a guarantee of a particular total-memory envelope.
2. Consider optional pending-output byte/count limits for applications that cannot apply producer backpressure. Specify rejection, callback delivery and cancellation ownership first; include WebSocket sends and SSE. Connection admission is now available, with an unlimited default; choosing a safe default or a 64 MiB configuration still requires measurement.
3. Bound or coalesce HTTP/2 coordinator commands without blocking the reader behind a flow-controlled writer. Preserve control-frame progress, reset ordering and shutdown completion.
4. Measure a selected configuration's total-memory envelope and sustained recovery when the host is available. This audit does not establish Mu3/Mu4 performance parity or release acceptance.

## Verification

The asynchronous `TransportOutputBuffer` path borrows one source until transport
acknowledgement and drains bounded views directly to the socket/TLS sink. It no
longer copies that source into its ring. The existing 8,201-byte ring allocation
is still present for the blocking bridge; source retention and queue policy are unchanged. Borrowed async drains have a
separate 16,393-byte limit; blocking drains remain capped by the ring. The
connection also preserves the previous maximum plaintext per owner visit
(16 × 8,201 bytes), so the larger individual calls do not double that maximum. `TransportAsyncOutputTest` verifies shared backing
storage, direct/read-only and sliced buffers, zero/partial drains, and source
ownership through failure. `TlsEngineDriverTest` checks that stalled ciphertext
and changing drain budgets cannot acknowledge plaintext early.

`AsyncResponseOutputTest` checks success, failure, cancellation and rejected writes for retained Mu-owned references, including delayed callback dispatch. Its object graph inspection follows Mu fields and actual collections; it does not depend on garbage-collection timing or open JDK internals. Existing cases verify ordering, exactly-once delivery, and buffer ownership until I/O acknowledges abort.

`AsyncOutputBufferTest` checks empty, small and multi-chunk array/direct/read-only buffers, slice offsets, exact bytes, bounded scratch-array size and a single flush. `AsyncOutputIntegrationTest` sends multi-chunk payloads over HTTP/1 and HTTP/2 with one application worker, plus cancellation of flow-controlled output. Existing `LifecycleRecoveryTest` covers slow-peer disconnect, timeout and shutdown retirement. These are small correctness tests, not performance or capacity tests.

Rate-limit cleanup is covered by `RateLimiterCleanupTest`: fake-clock expiry without a further request/query, signed nano-time wraparound, preservation of live and concurrently renewed buckets, other-request progress between bounded batches, one coalesced task for multiple limiters, executor rejection recovery, no timer for an unconfigured server, and shutdown of queued/running cleanup. Existing `RateLimiterTest` covers atomic decisions and HTTP/1/HTTP/2 behavior.

## Configuring connection admission

Set `withMaxConnections(n)` on the server builder to bound admitted connections across every HTTP and HTTPS listener. Zero is the unlimited default; negative values are rejected. Choose `n` from the deployment budget and workload, separately from `withMaxConcurrentRequests(n)`. An HTTP/2 connection uses one connection slot even with multiple active streams. A WebSocket keeps its slot after the upgrade request finishes. Setup sockets count before PROXY or TLS negotiation, including time spent waiting for delegated TLS tasks.

`withListenBacklog(n)` separately sets the requested OS queue length on each HTTP/HTTPS listening socket. The default remains 50; values must be positive. The getter reports the requested value, not a measured queue capacity. [Java documents the effective backlog as implementation-dependent](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/net/ServerSocket.html): it may be capped or ignored. This is a queue of connections waiting for acceptance, not the count already admitted by Mu and not a socket receive/send buffer size.

At capacity, the acceptor waits on a condition without holding a lock across socket I/O. Waiting before `accept()` does not reserve capacity: an idle HTTPS port cannot take the last slot away from HTTP. A shared atomic admission decision after acceptance closes any excess candidate before setup when listeners race. Overload may therefore produce connection delay, refusal/reset or timeout, rather than HTTP 503. `rejectedDueToOverload()` counts candidates actually rejected by Mu, not clients waiting in the OS backlog. Admission is released after socket closure and cleanup; timed-out setup and late worker retirement cannot release the same slot twice. This bounds admitted sockets, not all application tasks or total memory.

`ConnectionAdmissionTest` verifies concurrent last-slot admission, non-reserving waits, idempotent retirement, interruption and listener/server stop signals. `ConnectionLimitTest` checks idle HTTP/HTTPS reuse, silent TLS setup, shared ports, simultaneous HTTP/2 streams, WS/WSS upgrades, idle and PROXY setup timeouts, disconnect and shutdown. `ConnectionRejectionTest` checks internal executor rejection and recovery with both unlimited and capped admission. These bounded checks make no performance or capacity claim.
